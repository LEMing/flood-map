What Floodlab actually computes

Not a flood-risk report. A transparent physics sandbox.

The goal is not to pretend we know every pipe, curb, inlet, and soil layer. The goal is to make the assumptions visible and let people feel how terrain, rainfall, roughness, losses, and drainage change the flood pattern.

01 / terrain

Metric local world

A place becomes a local projected grid in metres, so water movement is calculated in real distances instead of screen pixels.

02 / surface

Elevation, buildings, soil

Open elevation, OSM features, land cover and mapped soil texture feed the terrain, roughness, infiltration and urban drainage assumptions.

03 / storm

Rain is a curve, not a slider

Storm presets use time-varying hyetographs, so peak rainfall can arrive after the ground is already wet and low points are filling.

04 / flow

Water moves cell by cell

Each grid cell stores water depth, while cell faces store discharge. The model evolves flow, then updates depth by continuity.

Physics under the hood

The math is shallow-water, local-inertial, and intentionally inspectable.

Water surface

The solver works on η = z + h: terrain elevation plus water depth. Flow follows differences in this surface, not just the ground slope.

Momentum on faces

East and north faces carry discharge q, so a flood wave can accelerate, overshoot, slow down, and reverse.

Friction and losses

Manning roughness, infiltration, evaporation and synthetic storm-sewer capacity pull water out of overland flow in controlled, visible ways.

Stable time steps

Substeps obey a CFL-style limit, roughly Δt ≤ Δx / (|v| + √gh), so fast shallow flow gets smaller steps.

Calculation facts

Every scenario is measured, not just rendered.

Grid resolution

Choose 256–2048 cells per side. Higher grids reveal smaller flow paths but take longer to build and simulate.

Mass balance

Rain in, water stored, flooded area and peak depth are all tracked, so scenarios are measurable instead of just visual.

GPU-first

The heavy flow fields run on WebGL float textures, so the UI stays interactive while terrain, water and overlays update.

Verified core

The solver is checked against closed-form shallow-water cases and a CPU reference, so the numbers stay defensible.

Questions people ask first

Floodlab FAQ

Clear enough for a curious homeowner, explicit enough for an engineer to spot the assumptions.

Is Floodlab a real forecast?

No. It is an educational simulation with explicit assumptions, not a calibrated emergency forecast or engineering flood study.

Can I simulate any address?

Yes. Enter an address, place name, or coordinates, then Floodlab prepares terrain and open-data layers for that local area.

What makes the water feel realistic?

The simulation stores momentum on grid faces, so water does not just slide downhill. It can surge, pond, drain, and reverse as the surface changes.

Can I export a flood video?

On supported browsers, Floodlab can render a short cinematic flood video for the selected location.
